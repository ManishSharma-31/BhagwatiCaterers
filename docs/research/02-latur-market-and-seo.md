## 4. Customer behaviour and the quote flow

[Mostly INFERRED from general Indian SMB practice; I did not find Latur-specific behaviour studies. Validate with owner.]
- WhatsApp-first: customers expect to send date, venue, guest count, and get a menu PDF/photo and rate by WhatsApp. Phone calls remain primary for older/rural customers and for family decision-makers; show both call and WhatsApp buttons prominently, with call-back-request option.
- Per-plate (per head) is the standard quoting unit for buffet/pangat events; small orders are by kg/tray/item. Quotes often have a minimum guest count, extra-plate rate, and tax/service line items.
- Payments: UPI (PhonePe/GPay/Paytm) plus cash; show UPI QR on invoice/site payment info page. WeddingWire listing shows competitor accepts cash, cheque, cards, UPI.
- Advance norms: a sample Indian caterer's published terms ask 25% (or Rs 5,000 minimum) at booking, about 80% 15 days before, rest on the event day, and 15 days' notice for cancellation: https://sites.google.com/view/sanjaycaterer/terms-conditions (one caterer, outside Latur; example only). Consumer-forum guidance on advances/refunds: https://righttoinformation.wiki/event-decorator-caterer-advance-refund-india (not read in full). Owner must decide own terms; publish them clearly (a differentiator because aggregator listings hide them).

### The two order types - how to present
Name them plainly, in Marathi first:
- "साहित्यासह (Full service / Without your material)": we supply all ingredients, cooking, staff, serving; priced per plate (list what is included: crockery, water, serving staff, decoration, transport; and what is extra).
- "आपल्या साहित्यासह (With your material / Cook-only)": customer supplies raw material; we supply cooks, equipment/utensils (if yes), and labour; priced per plate/per kg of cooked food/per cook-day (owner to decide). Needs clarity on who buys, wastage responsibility, quantity guidance ("how much to buy for N guests" calculator is a strong helper), and food-safety responsibility for customer-supplied ingredients.
Wording caution: the owner's phrase "with material/without material" is ambiguous in Marathi/English; use a comparison table with 3 columns: Who buys ingredients / Who cooks and serves / How price is calculated, plus short icons. Avoid ambiguity: label by action ("We bring everything" vs "You provide ingredients, we cook").

Quote form fields (short, mobile-friendly, WhatsApp submit fallback): name; mobile (WhatsApp same?); occasion (dropdown); event date + time (and flexible?); venue/town; approx guests (range) + veg-jain/no onion-garlic options (fasting/upvas options); order type (two big cards); cuisine preference (Maharashtrian/South Indian/Rajasthani/mix); service style (pangat/buffet/packed box/home delivery); extras (utensils, decor, waiters, tent? per owner offering); budget per plate (optional); preferred contact language. On submit: auto-open WhatsApp message with filled details + save to Google Sheet/email. Show "reply within X hours" promise only if owner can keep it.
Language: Marathi default; toggle EN/HI persists in cookie; all form labels translated; do not machine-translate menu names (keep Marathi dish names with English transliteration).

## 5. FSSAI (NOT LEGAL ADVICE; confirm with an FSSAI consultant or the district Food Safety Officer, Latur)

- Licence number on documents: FSSAI order (June 2021, enforcement date moved to 1 Jan 2022) requires food business operators to print the 14-digit licence/registration number on cash receipts, invoices, cash memos, bills and similar transaction documents. Source (FSSAI PDF): https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/advisories/2021/10/6156936ac80bbOrder_License_Number_Print_01_10_2021.pdf . News/explainers: https://www.livelaw.in/columns/food-safety-and-standards-authority-of-india-fssai-fss-licensing-and-registration-of-food-businesses-regulations-2011-food-safety-and-standards-act-act-176022 , https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/fssai-food-businesses-mention-license-no-invoices-w-e-f-1st-october-2021.html
  => Put the licence number on every quote, invoice, WhatsApp-sent bill and receipt, and on the website footer/about page (website display itself is not specifically mandated for caterers in the sources I found, but it is consistent with the intent and a trust signal).
- E-commerce rule: FSSAI e-commerce advisories require platforms/ e-commerce FBOs to display licence number on their platform; relevant only if the site takes online orders/payments and sells food (check). Sources: https://food.chemlinked.com/news/food-news/fssai-imposes-stringent-food-safety-mandates-on-e-commerce-platforms-across-india , https://livelaw.in/consumer-cases/fssai-issues-advisory-for-e-commerce-food-business-operators-to-ensure-consumer-protection-279669 (search snippets only, not read in full).
- Licence tier for caterers: by turnover: registration up to Rs 12 lakh, state licence Rs 12 lakh to Rs 20 crore, per https://www.indiafilings.com/fssai/catering (commercial site; confirm against FSSAI https://www.fssai.gov.in). FSSAI thresholds were reported raised recently (https://ssrana.in/?p=44449 - not read); verify current limits.
- Food Safety Display Board (FSDB): FSSAI has a scheme for displaying licence/hygiene information at premises (https://fssai.gov.in/cms/food-safety-display-boards.php - page did not load content for me). Ask the owner whether the kitchen displays a licence copy.
- Veg / non-veg logo (green dot in square): the FSS (Labelling and Display) Regulations 2020 require veg/non-veg logo on packaged food labels (FSSAI FAQ: https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQ_Labelling_Display_23_06_2022.pdf). A Business Standard explainer (Sept 2022) on menus said restaurant menus must mark each item veg/non-veg and flag allergens, but the fetch could not confirm a calorie/FSSAI-number rule for restaurants: https://business-standard.com/amp/article/current-affairs/fssai-rules-here-is-what-packaged-food-and-restaurant-menus-must-mention-122091200740_1.html . I could not verify a binding requirement for catering websites. Practical approach: show the green-dot icon beside every menu item or a single "100% pure vegetarian kitchen" statement; do not use the FSSAI logo as decoration or imply FSSAI endorsement.
- Claims: FSSAI has a 2018 Advertising and Claims regulation and has acted against misleading food claims (https://fssai.gov.in/upload/media/fssaimisleading.pdf ; https://www.managingip.com/article/2a5bqtj8ume32iwlaoy4c/india-fssai-steps-in-to-regulate-advertising-for-food-business-operators). Safe approach: only state verifiable facts (pure veg, no onion-garlic option if true, FSSAI licence number, years in business); avoid "best", "100% safe", "healthy", "organic", "no preservatives/adulteration-free" or medical/health claims unless provable; avoid "hygiene certified" unless the certificate (e.g. FoSTaC-trained supervisor, Hygiene Rating audit) exists and can be shown. Consumer Protection Act 2019 and CCPA misleading-ad rules also apply [INFERRED].
- FoSTaC: caterers must have trained food safety supervisor per FSSAI (training certificate) - ask owner [INFERRED; verify].
- Photographs/testimonials need consent (DPDP Act 2023 for personal data in forms) [INFERRED]; add privacy notice.
